# 2026-10-20 Tuesday

## Boot up, go idle

Since I am starting this initiative today, I need to explain what is actual goal
of this approach.

My work recently does not give me a feel of purpose. Recent changes in AI
development and speeding up the process of creating software in general caused
that brains of the actual creators are rotting due to large amount of things
they should care of and yet a little of creative purpose.

Basically, I am a decision-making engine on top of one of LLMs in order to keep
up with the pace requested by my employer.

This situation makes me think: do I create anything that is mine? Some people
would say: yes, eventually those decisions are yours. But I have a feeling that
I don't know the solutions I ma creating by heart and there is no way to get
that familiar with them.

Makes me frustrated, I feel that I am wasting my life. Back in the days, I was
responsible, thorough, smart engineer. Maybe I wasn't but at least I am thinking
about this as one.

The goal here is to steam out, to write thoughts that are meaningful to me,
despite topics, despite that I am a software engineer or a human being, even. I
want to be free.

As Nikos Kazantzakis wrote (which is my best writer discovery so far):

> I expect nothing. I fear nothing. I am free.

And I find this refreshing. Well, that sentence wouldn't mean much to someone
that haven't read at least Zorba the Greek. I will not describe the story here
as it will require at least dozen pages, but enough to say that he has seen the
wilderness of life and its injustice.

## Why do we live?

This is a question I have been asking myself for a long while. There is no
reason we exist. This has been properly diagnosed by Leo Tolstoy in his
Confession, altough I don't agree God is the answer to fill this emptiness of
purpose. On the other hand, I think God can be used in a matter of philosophy,
but not as the answer, but as the blueprint, I will perhaps explain this someday
in this diary.

## Istanbul - The City

Interestingly, I am going with my teammates to offsite this week - we are going
to visit Istanbul. I don't know what cheers me more - whether is seeing my
collegaues I work with every day or seeing the City the first time, after my
period of deep, merely obsessed, interest in Byzantine Empire (also known as
Romaioi) and Greek culture/language.

I love my collegaues, they are people that live the full life, as opposed to me.
There would be drinks, discussions and friendly gestures that I hope will be a
better way of saying "thank you" or "I am glad you're helping me this whole
time", because I do appreciate we are facing the same void and we can deal with
it, toghether. What I expect to get out of this two days is deep conversations
about life, about our work and about us, without judgement, without fear and any
correction. This is the goal, let's see how we are going to achieve this.

On the other hand - the City. Oh, I have heard so much about you! Unjustly taken
away from the Empire it never get back to Greek-speaking culture, even so being
its capital for thousand years. Center of ancient culture preservation in
medival times, a place where death penalty was not as common ans in other
places, multicultural and multilingual, this city shines to me in my imagination
as the cornerstone of current Western culture. I know, it was not that colorful
and just place in the world, it also had poverty and violence, however it was
different place compared to other cities in the world. I want to walk the same
roads Romaioi were walking, where Aristotle was taught for thousand years, and
Orthodox christianity spread to the north of Europe and Asia.
