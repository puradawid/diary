## Tolstoy

I finished reading Tolstoy's "Confession". Well, I didn't expect that bit.

So, Leo built the book on three stages. Three of those were a bit surprising,
one after another.

The first one says "there is a problem with life" which he defines as follows:
life has no meaning if there is no greater purpose. I am afraid I will kill
myself because of life without meaning makes no sense.

Completely right. He adds to this message some fantastic image of a man hanging
on a root in a well at the bottom of which there is a dragon wants to eat him...
Anyway, a bit spastic to my taste, although understandable, it was the 19th
century after all.

Anyway, I did agree with this perspective. But there is a second one...

The second stage goes "the common people know the remedy". You ask what this
knowledge is? Well, it's faith. No shit. God basically is the answer. God let
you live longer with happiness, despite the fact that you will die and nobody
will remember you eventually and life in general makes no sense. He keeps saying
that the ordinary people had obtained this knowledge eons ago and it naturally
keeps them alive.

What freaking bullshit. I don't want to go over every detail of this, but
basically I see no reason to call common people happy in life. They are also as
sad, frustrated, sick, abandoned and suicidal as educated people. There is no
difference in this, maybe educated ones have fewer problems in terms of poverty.

So, I thought I would give up at this point. But when I was lying down on my bed
at 4 AM, I decided to open the book again. And then I saw the last stage, and it
was miraculously different.

So, Leo goes like this: God - yes, church - no. Reason: the church has very
different goals than the faith itself. They are about maintaining continuity of
submission, not God's love. He gives these three examples:

1. Other Christians, like Protestants or Catholics, are named as completely
   misled people and only the Orthodox church is correct and the only true one.
   There is no dialogue, only hatred between believers. There is no salvation
   outside of the Orthodox church.
2. War, namely killing other people, is allowed and priests are praying for
   weapons and for the killing of other nations.
3. All those ceremonies - like the Eucharist - are pretty artificial and
   unnecessarily mysterious.

I do agree with him. But I have no more energy to discuss this, even internally.
