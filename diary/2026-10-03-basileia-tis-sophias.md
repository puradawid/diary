# 2026-11-03 Tuesday

Today, I am working on a concept that I have been thinking about for a long time
since I read Judith Herrin's book about Byzantium. She planted the idea in me
that there was an empire that kept Aristotle's treatises and other ancient
knowledge. I saw the Empire as the only enlightened place in medieval Europe.

But this vision is far from the truth. The Byzantine Empire was extremely
religious, hardly modernised, with a common conviction that praying to God
solves all problems. This is not exactly how I would imagine the perfect empire.

Thus, my vision has no real-world counterpart. In the first place, I just wept
because of the fall of Constantinople in 1453. Now, I think the
enlightened-empire idea deserves a better foundation: a story - an alternative
one that builds the right empire at the right place and time, protecting itself
from external threats such as the Seljuk Turks and internal ones, such as the
lack of modernisation of acquired ancient knowledge.

Here's the alternative story: the Empire founds the Academy in the 1000s, way
before the Crusaders sacking the City in 1204. The Academy teaches modernised
ancient subjects, like rhetoric or whatever else is there, producing good
quality clerks and strategists.

Since then, many Academy graduates are taking important roles in the Empire:
they are generals or high-ranked clerks. Both the Academy and the graduates are
keeping strong bond to each other, using knowledge as a tool for increasing the
greatness of the Empire itself.

When the Crusaders come in 1204 to Constantinople, Alexios III knows already
they are empty-handed, hungry and in debt. He is not a very wise emperor, but he
sends his best general to solve the problem.

The Crusaders are getting paid, equipped, and they helped the Empire push back
the Turks on the East. In effect, the Empire gets a little stronger so it can
stand until they change their Orthodox philosophy to a way of thinking in which
God is the Truth, the Logos, and the ultimate goal of the Empire is to examine
the reality and get closer to God - by redefining Theosis.

This story represents the main idea I have in my head.
